# Make every study session lead to the next step

Start with a textbook, a question, or a lesson you want to learn. This guide explains what you can do in Next Tutor Agent and how to continue your learning across sessions.

> New here? Start with “Quick start”. If you already have a goal, use the contents to find a relevant task. Some features need to be enabled by an administrator; follow the messages shown on your instance.

## Quick start

1. **Sign in to your account.** Keep your conversations, lessons, and notes so you can continue later.
2. **Prepare your materials.** Open Resources to choose public textbooks or upload your own. Associate the materials with the workspace you plan to use.
3. **Start learning.** Ask a specific question in Chat Tutoring, or prepare a lesson in Courses to study a topic in sequence.
4. **Save what you learn.** Try a practice question, turn useful explanations into notes, and check your records on the Dashboard.

You can begin with one textbook and one question.

## Chat Tutoring: work through a question

Open Chat Tutoring and choose a workspace and conversation. Describe what you are studying, where you are stuck, and the kind of explanation you need.

Try these prompts:

- **Understand a concept:** “Using this textbook, explain derivatives with an everyday example.”
- **Follow the reasoning:** “Why is this transformation valid? Please fill in the intermediate steps.”
- **Ask for a hint:** “Give me a hint for the first step before showing the answer.”
- **Check your approach:** “Here is my solution. Point out the step I should reconsider and let me correct it.”
- **Practise as you learn:** “Give me a question about this concept and wait for my answer before explaining.”

You can also upload a picture of a question. Image recognition depends on the instance configuration. Use a clear, complete image that includes the question and diagram labels.

For textbook-based answers, associate the textbook first and mention it in your question. Open source references to check the original material. If there is insufficient evidence, add a chapter, page number, or excerpt.

### Use voice

When the conversation shows a voice control, allow microphone access, hold to speak, and release to send. Follow the text and formulas as you listen. You can stop playback and return to typing.

Voice availability depends on your browser and instance configuration.

## Courses: turn a chapter into a lesson

Use Courses to study a topic continuously or prepare material for teaching.

1. Open Courses and select or create a workspace.
2. Select chapters from associated textbooks, or enter a topic. When you have no textbook, use the general-material option shown in the form.
3. Add your requirements and start preparation. Wait for the slides and script to be generated.
4. Open the lesson, review the slides, and adjust its content and script before starting.
5. Follow the slides and script during class. Resume unfinished lessons from the lesson list.

Download slides and scripts using the lesson’s export controls. Voice teaching needs an available speech service; the slides and script remain readable when speech is unavailable.

> A specific goal helps: “Prepare an introductory lesson on Newton’s second law. Explain the concept first, then add a force-analysis exercise.”

## Resources: learn from your materials

Resources stores textbooks and learning files. Public textbooks are shared for reading; personal uploads belong to your account.

- **Use an existing textbook:** Browse public textbooks and associate a suitable subject and edition with your workspace.
- **Upload your own:** Open the textbook upload form, enter the requested information, select files, and watch their processing status.
- **Organize learning topics:** Create separate workspaces for subjects or goals and associate the relevant materials with each.

Processing takes time. You can start asking questions once retrieval is ready, even while the knowledge map is still building. Scan clarity, page count, and model availability affect processing time.

## Practice and Assessment: check your understanding

Ask for practice questions in chat. If you have a reference problem, ask for a variation that tests the same concept, then try answering independently.

For a focused check, open Assessment:

1. Choose a workspace and concepts, then set the subject and grade level as needed.
2. Start the assessment, read each question, and submit your answer. Include your reasoning for written answers.
3. Read the feedback and explanation before continuing. Difficulty adapts to your answers in this session.
4. Review the summary and return to chat or practice to address remaining questions.

Recent questions and the error notebook help you review and retry. Enable illustrations when they are useful; some questions do not need diagrams.

An assessment summary describes performance on that session’s tasks. Answers completed after hints or revealed solutions should be interpreted with their assistance conditions.

## Tool assistants and the teaching atlas: picture the abstract

Open Tool assistants to create teaching diagrams with scene illustration:

1. Start an illustration session and describe the scene — subjects, relationships, labels, and text.
2. Choose a mode: draw freely from your description, assemble materials using their parameters and relationships, or use asset-assisted creation to adapt and combine selected references and draw missing elements. You can also pick reference materials manually; without a selection, the model searches for suitable materials automatically.
3. Refine the result through conversation. Each round is saved as a new version, and any earlier version can become the base for further refinement.
4. Download the version you like as an SVG for handouts, slides, or notes.

The Teaching atlas collects the project’s original teaching SVGs. Browse by subject and asset type, or search by name and alias. Open an asset to zoom in, adjust parameters and colors, and reuse it as an illustration reference or question artwork.

Illustration availability depends on the instance configuration; when the page says it is not enabled, the atlas remains available for browsing.

## Notes: keep your own understanding

Create a note to record concepts, solution steps, and questions. You can also generate notes from conversations, textbooks, or mistakes, then review and revise them in your own words.

- **Organize by topic:** Use folders and tags for courses, chapters, and material to review.
- **Connect ideas:** Add `[[note title]]` links between related notes.
- **Schedule reviews:** Add material you want to revisit and follow the due-review schedule.
- **Recover deleted notes:** Check recoverable items in the Archive Center.

A useful note can answer three questions: What did I understand? Where might I make a mistake? How will I check myself next time?

## Dashboard and Knowledge Map: choose what to study next

The Dashboard brings together learning records, evaluations, and tasks. The Knowledge Map lets you explore textbook concepts and their connections. Open a concept to review its information and ask a focused question.

When reading an evaluation, consider the performance and conditions behind it:

| Status | How to interpret it |
| --- | --- |
| No learning evidence yet | Try answering a question or explaining the concept to provide evidence. |
| Some local evidence | You have demonstrated part of your understanding; add more practice. |
| Supported within the current scope | Performance was good on the observed tasks and with the recorded assistance. Try another context. |
| Unresolved points or conflicting evidence | Revisit the relevant answers and practise the difficult steps. |

Open My account in the top bar for Account details, Learning Profile, Insights, and sign-in or sign-out. Account details shows your personal information, registration date and last login. Learning Profile and Memory show recorded learning information. New accounts need time to accumulate evidence.

Preferences live in Settings to the left of the account avatar in the top bar, grouped into General, Learning & answers, Voice & classroom, Assistant, and Document processing. Account deletion is under Account & data. Learning Overview remains in the left navigation. Theme can follow your system. Set the default stage in Learning & answers; it is saved to your account and applies to new chats. Automatic voice prefers local speech. About shows cloud and local voice model configuration. Upload and crop your avatar in Account details; deleting the account also deletes its avatar.

## Learning Orchestration: bring goals into your routine

Add a clear goal, such as “Review basic derivatives over two weeks and solve typical problems independently”. Use the weekly plan and daily tasks to begin studying and update progress as you go.

Include due reviews in your routine. After finishing a task, use practice feedback to decide what to reinforce. Marking a task complete records progress.

## Learning Assistant: find your starting point

When the Learning Assistant appears in the corner, open it to ask:

- “What can this website help me do?”
- “Take me to Courses.”
- “How has my learning gone this week?”
- “Continue my last lesson.”

Follow its prompts to confirm actions and choose the relevant lesson. Use Chat Tutoring for detailed explanations and practice. Open evidence links in reports to inspect the supporting records.

## Common questions

### Why can’t I find a textbook after uploading it?

Check its processing status in Resources. If it is ready, confirm that it is associated with the current workspace. Include the chapter or an excerpt to help locate the material.

### Why are voice, Courses, or the Assistant unavailable?

Availability depends on the instance configuration, service readiness, and browser support. Follow the page message or contact the administrator.

### How can I get an explanation that suits me?

Describe your background, the exact difficulty, and your preferred approach. For example: “I am new to this concept. Start with an intuitive picture and introduce formulas later.” Ask follow-up questions about steps you do not understand.

### Can everything I delete be restored?

Read the deletion prompt. Content moved to the Archive Center can be restored according to its retention policy. Permanent deletion and account deletion cannot be reversed through the archive.

### How do I change the language and theme?

Open Settings to the left of the account avatar in the top bar. General contains language and theme controls; Learning & answers contains Answer language, which controls model replies separately from the interface language.
